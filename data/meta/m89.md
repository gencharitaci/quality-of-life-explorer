## Heat Index
Heat Index


### Why is this important?
Heat is [dangerous] (https://www.cdc.gov/heat-health/about/index.html?CDC_AA_refVal=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.cdc.gov%2Fextreme-heat%2Fabout%2Findex.html).  In fact, it is the [leading cause of weather-related mortality worldwide] (https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/climate-change-heat-and-health).  And it is [getting worse] (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40599-x).

People at elevated risk from extreme heat include older adults, children, low-income communities, people with chronic health conditions, those working or playing outside, people without air conditioning, and those who are pregnant, according to the [National Integrated Heat Health Information Systems] (https://www.heat.gov/pages/who-is-at-risk-to-extreme-heat).

Understanding where it is hottest in Charlotte is crucial for mitigating the impacts of extreme heat on the most vulnerable people in our community.

### Did you know?
The Urban Heat Island Effect is caused by a concentration of common building materials like asphalt, concrete, steel, as well as a lack of vegetation and tree canopy.  These materials absorb and store heat, which increases the temperature on the ground.  Vegetation, particularly trees, can have a cooling effect.

Neighborhoods with more amenities such as parks, greenways, open space, and trees generally have lower temperatures.  Hotter areas include industrial corridors, freeways, and areas with broad expanses of asphalt.

### How Are We Doing?
All NPAs are hot during the summer in Charlotte, but some are hotter than others, as this map displays.  Charlotte has witnessed an increasing number of high heat days over the last 30 years, with Average, Minimum and Maximum temperatures all having climbed approximately .5 degrees Celsius per decade during the summer months.

### About the Data
This map shows average heat index across NPAs.  Heat Index is a compound measure of both relative humidity and temperature.  Including humidity in the metric provides a more realistic sense of how heat feels than measuring temperature alone.  Geolocated point data was gathered using car-mounted mobile sensors by community volunteers.
